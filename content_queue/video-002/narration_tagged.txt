[M] A sunk cost is money, time, or effort you've already spent and can never get back, no matter what you do next.
[M] The fallacy is treating that unrecoverable cost as a reason to keep going, instead of only looking at what happens from this point forward.
[F] Economists have known about this for decades.
[M] The classic example is a government project.

[M] The rational move is to stop immediately.
[M] The fifty million already spent is gone either way.
[M] But people keep building it anyway, because stopping feels like admitting the first fifty million was wasted.
[F] Finishing doesn't undo that waste.

[M] Admitting a sunk cost was wasted feels like admitting you were wrong.
